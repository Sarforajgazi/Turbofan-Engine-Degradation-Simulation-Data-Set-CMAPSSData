Roadmap
1. EDA / understanding the signal
Load FD001 first (single condition, single fault — simplest case)
Plot a few engines' sensor trajectories over their lifetime to see degradation trends
Identify near-constant sensors (several of the 21 barely vary in FD001 — e.g., sensors 1, 5, 6, 10, 16, 18, 19) — decide whether to keep or drop them
Check sensor correlations (many are physically coupled, e.g., T30/T50/P30)
2. Build RUL labels
Training set: RUL at each row = (max cycle for that engine) − (current cycle)
Apply the standard piecewise-linear cap (e.g., clip RUL at 125 cycles) since engines are healthy early on and RUL isn't meaningfully predictable from sensors before degradation starts
Test set: use the provided RUL_FD001.txt as ground truth for the final cycle of each test engine
3. Preprocessing
Normalize/scale sensors (z-score is standard); for FD002/FD004, normalize within each of the 6 operating regimes separately, since raw values shift with condition
Drop or flag near-constant sensors
4. Design your missingness experiment (this is the imputation-specific part)
Choose a mechanism: MCAR (random points), MAR (condition-dependent), MNAR (fails more near end-of-life), or block/sequence dropout (contiguous cycles missing — most realistic for real sensor outages)
Choose a missingness rate (e.g., 5%, 10%, 20%) so you can see how imputation quality degrades
Hold out the true values before masking so you have ground truth to score against
5. Impute
Baselines: forward-fill, linear interpolation, mean/median
Stronger: KNN imputation, MICE (multiple imputation by chained equations)
Sequence-aware: LSTM/GRU autoencoder or a simple denoising model, since this is time-series and neighboring cycles carry strong signal
6. Evaluate imputation itself
RMSE/MAE between imputed and true values, per sensor and per missingness rate/mechanism
7. Evaluate downstream impact (the more interesting part)
Train your RUL model (LSTM/GRU/CNN/Transformer or even windowed gradient boosting) on clean data → get baseline RMSE/score
Retrain/re-evaluate with imputed data at each missingness rate → see how much RUL prediction degrades
This tells you whether imputation quality actually matters for the task, not just in isolation
8. RUL modeling (main event, once imputation pipeline is validated)
Sliding-window sequences per engine (fixed window length or full sequence with padding/masking)
Model options: LSTM/GRU, 1D-CNN, Transformer encoder, or simpler windowed feature + XGBoost
Evaluate with RMSE and the paper's asymmetric scoring function (Eq. 11 — penalizes late predictions more than early ones)
